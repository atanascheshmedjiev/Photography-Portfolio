The Proposal

Planned ahead, shot from a distance with a long lens, so the moment stayed theirs.

The light lasted about forty minutes. The question took one. Everything after it was the two of them forgetting the camera was there.

Opposite: 200mm, f/4, 1/500s, ISO 200

Eye Level

Most of these were made at 200mm, wide open, waiting for an animal to turn toward the one patch of light it had.

Octopus, puffins, sea lions, a crow, a rhino. I try to get down to their level and stay there until they look back.

Opposite: 174mm, f/4, 1/250s, ISO 6400

Small Figures

A lot of these frames have a person in them, and they are almost always tiny. I like what that does to the land: it stops being scenery and starts being a place someone is standing in.

Canyons, coastline, desert and forest, photographed over several years of travelling, mostly with one camera and one lens.

Opposite: 50mm, f/7.1, 1/1250s, ISO 200

About

I mostly shoot with medium to long lenses and little light. Most of my favourite frames come from waiting: for the sun to drop, for an animal to turn its head, for two people to forget I am there.

I learned on a Canon 1200D with the 18-55mm f/3.5-5.6 kit lens, photographing whatever stood still long enough. These days it is mostly people: proposals, couples and portraits, plus the animals and landscapes I go looking for between sessions.

I like shadows more than most photographers do. I would rather leave something dark and let one face, one hand or one ridge catch the light than show everything at once.

What I carry

Canon EOS R6 Mark II, Canon EOS RP, Canon RF 70–200mm F4 L IS USM, Canon RF 50mm F1.8 STM.
